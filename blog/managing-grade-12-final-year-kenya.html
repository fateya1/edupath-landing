CBE and Student Welfare

Managing Grade 12 students in their final year — what principals need to know

June 5, 2026 | 7 min read | By Edupath SMS Team

Grade 12 is unlike any other year in a Kenyan senior school. The students are under the highest academic pressure of their lives, their parents are anxious and closely watching, teachers feel the weight of the school's reputation riding on results, and the principal is managing all of this simultaneously while keeping the rest of the school running normally.

Schools that handle Grade 12 well have learned to be deliberate about it — treating it as a distinct management challenge rather than just a more intense version of Grade 10 or 11. Here is what experienced principals have found works.

Get assessment records complete early

The single most important administrative task for Grade 12 is ensuring that every student has complete, accurate assessment records well before the end of the year. Under CBE, continuous assessment contributes to the final portfolio, meaning gaps or errors in CAT records can have serious consequences for a student's overall outcomes.

A digital system that shows at a glance which students have missing assessments in which subjects — and who the responsible teacher is — allows the deputy principal to chase gaps proactively in Term 2, not scramble to fix them in Term 3. By the time Term 3 begins, every Grade 12 student's CAT records should be complete and verified.

Manage attendance with extra vigilance

Grade 12 absenteeism is particularly damaging because missed lessons at this stage cannot easily be recovered. A student who misses two weeks of Physics in Term 2 may not have the foundation to succeed in the Term 3 assessments. Yet Grade 12 students are also the most likely to believe they can afford to skip school — they feel the end is in sight and the pressure paradoxically sometimes leads to avoidance.

Automated parent alerts for every absence are especially important for Grade 12. A parent who receives a WhatsApp message the moment their child is marked absent is in a position to intervene immediately. A parent who finds out three weeks later is not.

Watch for the mid-year dip

June and July are historically when Grade 12 attendance and engagement dip in Kenyan schools. Students who have been working hard since January begin to fatigue. This is a predictable pattern, and principals who anticipate it can put support structures in place before the dip happens rather than reacting to it afterwards.

Practical intervention: A brief one-on-one conversation between the class teacher and any Grade 12 student whose attendance has dropped below 90% in Term 2 — before it drops further — is one of the highest-return pastoral investments a school can make at this stage of the year.

Communicate clearly with Grade 12 parents

Grade 12 parents are the most engaged and the most anxious parent group in your school. They need more communication, not less. A termly update on their child's assessment progress, attendance record, and subject teacher's observations — delivered via the Parent Portal and summarised in a WhatsApp message — gives them the information they need and significantly reduces the volume of calls and gate visits your staff would otherwise receive.

Fee management for Grade 12

Fee arrears among Grade 12 families create a specific problem: a student withheld from sitting assessments due to unpaid fees in their final year faces consequences that extend well beyond the current term. Most schools have a policy that distinguishes between withholding report cards (acceptable) and withholding assessment access (not acceptable). Whatever your school's policy, it needs to be communicated clearly to Grade 12 parents at the start of the year — not at the point of enforcement.

Automated fee reminders and M-Pesa payment access significantly reduce the number of Grade 12 families who reach the end of term with large outstanding balances. The easier you make it to pay, the fewer crises you manage at result time.

Support your Grade 12 teachers

Teaching Grade 12 is stressful. Subject teachers feel personally responsible for their students' outcomes in a way that is not always healthy or sustainable. The principal's role includes checking in on Grade 12 teachers specifically — not to add pressure, but to acknowledge the weight they are carrying and ensure they have what they need. A teacher who feels seen and supported performs better than one who feels alone with the responsibility.

Keep your Grade 12 records complete and parents informed.
