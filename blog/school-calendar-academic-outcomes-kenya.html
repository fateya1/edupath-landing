How to use the school calendar to drive better academic outcomes

July 1, 2026 | 6 min read | By Edupath SMS Team

The school calendar is one of the most underutilised leadership tools available to a Kenyan principal. Most schools produce a calendar at the start of the year, display it on a notice board, and then manage the year reactively as events arrive. Schools that use the calendar strategically — planning assessment dates, parent engagement, fee collection windows, and staff development around the academic rhythm — consistently achieve better outcomes than those that do not.

Here is how to build and use a school calendar that actually drives performance.

Start with the academic skeleton

The foundation of a useful school calendar is the assessment schedule. Before adding any other events, plot every CAT1, CAT2, and End-Term assessment date for every year group across all three terms. These dates are non-negotiable anchors. Everything else in the calendar should be planned around them, not over them.

A common mistake is scheduling major school events — sports days, cultural festivals, parent meetings — in the two weeks before End-Term assessments. Students are distracted, teachers are pulled away from teaching, and results suffer. A calendar that builds a clear two-week preparation window before every major assessment avoids this entirely.

Align fee due dates with parent cash flow

Fee due dates set at the start of term without consideration of when parents actually have money are a primary driver of arrears. In communities where parents are paid monthly, a fee due date of the 5th of the first month of term will always produce lower on-time payment rates than a due date of the 1st — because many parents are paid at month end and need a few days to organise payment.

A calendar that sets fee due dates strategically — and sends automated reminders two weeks before the due date, one week before, and on the due date — collects more fees on time than one that sets dates arbitrarily and relies on parents to remember.

Schedule parent engagement proactively

Parent meetings that are called reactively — because of a problem, or because the term is ending and it seems like a good time — are attended poorly and achieve little. Parent meetings scheduled at the same time each term, communicated well in advance, and with a clear agenda that is relevant to parents are attended significantly better.

A calendar that includes one structured parent engagement event per term — at a consistent time, with adequate notice, covering assessment progress and upcoming school events — builds a culture of parent involvement that benefits the whole school.

What works: The most well-attended parent meetings in Kenyan schools are those scheduled for the Saturday before term begins. Parents are available, the topic — what to expect this term — is directly relevant to them, and attendance signals commitment to their child's education at a moment when it matters.

Block staff development into the calendar

Teacher professional development that happens only when there is a spare afternoon is professional development that rarely happens. A calendar that blocks one full staff development day per term — on a fixed date, known at the start of the year — is a calendar that treats teacher development as a priority rather than an aspiration.

For CBE specifically, a termly session on assessment methodology, competency-level descriptors, or pathway-specific content keeps teachers aligned and confident in a curriculum that is still relatively new for many of them.

Share the calendar with everyone — digitally

A calendar that only exists on the staffroom notice board serves only the people who read that notice board. A digital school calendar, accessible to teachers from the Teacher Portal and to parents from the Parent Portal, ensures that every stakeholder has access to the same information at all times. When an assessment date changes, one update in the system reaches everyone simultaneously. No reprinting, no re-posting, no confusion.
