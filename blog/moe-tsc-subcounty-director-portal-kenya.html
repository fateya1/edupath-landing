Regulation and Compliance

What the MOE and TSC Sub-County Director portal means for your school

July 1, 2026 | 7 min read | By Edupath SMS Team

The way Kenya's education regulators oversee schools is changing. Historically, oversight happened through periodic physical inspections — a SQASO visits, reviews paper records, writes a report, and leaves. The cycle could take years between meaningful contact points. Schools that were not compliant had little incentive to maintain records between visits.

Digital regulator portals change this fundamentally. When MOE Sub-County Directors and TSC Sub-County Directors have direct digital access to a school's data — enrolment numbers, attendance rates, assessment records, teacher deployment — oversight becomes continuous rather than periodic. For principals who run well-organised schools, this is a competitive advantage. For those who do not, it is a new source of accountability.

What the regulator portal provides access to

Edupath SMS's dedicated Regulator Portal gives verified MOE and TSC Sub-County Directors read-only access to aggregated school data across all schools in their jurisdiction. This includes:

  • Student enrolment numbers and CBE pathway distribution across their sub-county
  • School-wide attendance rates and trends over time
  • Teacher deployment data — subjects assigned, TSC numbers, and active status
  • Assessment completion rates and results summaries
  • Fee collection status at school level (not individual student data)
  • Exportable CSV reports for official reporting to county and national level

All regulator access is secured with two-factor authentication (2FA) and every action is logged in a full audit trail — so schools always know when their data has been reviewed and by whom.

What this means for school principals

Your records are always being assessed — not just during inspections

The shift from periodic to continuous oversight means that data quality matters every day, not just when an inspector is expected. A school with accurate, up-to-date attendance records, complete teacher profiles, and current assessment data will present well to a regulator at any point. A school with gaps, inconsistencies, or outdated records will be visible — without the warning that a scheduled inspection used to provide.

Proactive compliance becomes a differentiator

In a system where regulators can see school data at any time, principals who maintain compliance proactively — keeping records current, ensuring teacher TSC numbers are on file, maintaining attendance registers daily — will stand out positively from those who scramble. Sub-county directors will naturally develop a view of which schools in their jurisdiction are well-run and which are not, based on data rather than just inspection reports.

The opportunity this creates: A school principal who uses the same system as the regulator — and whose data is always accurate — is a principal who can have a very different conversation with their Sub-County Director. Instead of defending records, they are sharing performance data. That is a relationship-changing shift.

Multi-jurisdiction schools benefit from clearer oversight

For school networks or county-level boards managing multiple institutions, the regulator portal provides aggregated cross-school visibility. A Sub-County Director overseeing 40 schools in their jurisdiction can identify attendance outliers, enrolment anomalies, or assessment gaps across all schools simultaneously — enabling faster, more targeted support and intervention.

How to prepare your school for digital regulator access

The practical steps are straightforward but require consistency:

  • Ensure every student profile is complete — name, admission number, UPI, CBE pathway, class assignment
  • Ensure every teacher profile includes their TSC registration number and current subject assignments
  • Mark attendance daily in the digital system — gaps in the digital record are as visible to the regulator as gaps in a paper register
  • Keep assessment records current — enter results promptly after assessments are marked
  • Resolve any data inconsistencies now, before they become part of a regulatory conversation

Data privacy and security in the regulator portal

Regulator access is governed by Kenya's Data Protection Act (2019). The Edupath SMS Regulator Portal provides aggregated, anonymised data to regulators at the school level — individual student data is not exposed. All access is authenticated with 2FA, logged with timestamps and user IDs, and visible to the school's administrator. Schools retain control of their data and can see exactly what has been accessed and when.
