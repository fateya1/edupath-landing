Parent Engagement

How to run a parent-teacher meeting that parents actually attend

July 1, 2026 | 6 min read | By Edupath SMS Team

Low attendance at parent-teacher meetings is one of the most demoralising experiences in school leadership. You plan, prepare, and arrange the hall — and 30% of parents show up, mostly those whose children are already doing well. The parents of students who most need intervention are the ones least likely to appear.

This is not a Kenyan problem uniquely — it is a global one. But there are specific, evidence-backed approaches that consistently improve attendance and quality of parent-teacher engagement. Here is what works.

The three reasons parents do not come

Before fixing the problem, it helps to understand it. Parents who do not attend parent-teacher meetings typically fall into one of three categories:

  • Logistical barriers: The meeting is during working hours, there is no transport, or childcare is unavailable
  • Past experience: Previous meetings were unproductive, felt like lectures, or delivered bad news with no constructive outcome
  • Fear or shame: Parents whose children are struggling academically or behaviourally often avoid meetings because they anticipate criticism they feel unable to respond to

Each of these barriers requires a different response. Logistical barriers require scheduling and access solutions. Past experience barriers require a genuinely different meeting format. Fear and shame barriers require active outreach and a different tone.

Timing and logistics

Saturday morning meetings consistently outperform weekday afternoon meetings for attendance in Kenyan schools. Parents who work during the week can attend on Saturday. The school environment feels less formal. And the Saturday morning slot signals that the meeting is important enough to be held outside regular school hours.

Send the meeting notice three weeks in advance via WhatsApp — not a paper notice sent home with a student who may or may not deliver it. Include the date, time, location, and a one-line agenda. Follow up with a reminder one week before and another the day before. Schools that send three reminders consistently report 20-30% higher attendance than those that send one.

Redesign the meeting format

The traditional parent-teacher meeting format — a one-way presentation by the principal followed by questions — is the format least likely to produce parent engagement. Parents sit passively, receive information they may not fully understand, and leave feeling no more connected to the school than when they arrived.

What works better: structured one-on-one slots

Rather than a general meeting, schedule 10-minute individual slots between each parent and their child's class teacher or subject teachers. Parents know exactly what time to arrive, have a specific conversation rather than a general one, and leave with information relevant to their child. The logistics require more planning but the quality of engagement is dramatically higher.

Lead with the positive

Every parent conversation should begin with something genuine and specific that their child is doing well. A parent who feels the school values their child is a parent who is open to hearing about areas for improvement. A parent who feels the school only contacts them about problems becomes defensive and disengaged.

The single most effective change: Ask parents a question and listen to the answer. "What does your child say about school at home?" or "Is there anything happening at home we should know about that might be affecting your child's performance?" Parents who feel heard at a meeting tell other parents. Attendance grows through reputation.

For parents who never attend

Some parents will not attend regardless of how the meeting is designed. For these families, a brief WhatsApp voice note from the class teacher — personal, specific, and two minutes long — is often more effective than any formal meeting. It reaches the parent where they are, requires no travel, and can be listened to at a convenient time. A Parent Portal that gives these parents access to their child's attendance, results, and fee statement means they can stay informed even without attending meetings.

Keep parents engaged all year, not just at meetings.
