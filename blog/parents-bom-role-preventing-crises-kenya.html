The role of parents and the BOM in preventing school crises

August 3, 2026 | 6 min read | By Edupath SMS Team

School crises are sometimes discussed as though they are purely a matter of student behaviour and school administration response. In practice, a genuinely resilient school treats parents and the Board of Management as active partners in prevention — not passive recipients of information after something has already gone wrong. This guide covers how to build that partnership meaningfully.

The BOM's governance role in prevention

The Basic Education Act (2013) establishes the BOM as ultimately responsible for school governance, including student welfare and safety standards. A BOM that meets only to approve budgets and rarely engages with student welfare matters is not fulfilling this role fully. BOMs that are genuinely engaged in prevention typically receive a termly report from the principal covering not just academic performance but attendance trends, discipline patterns, and any emerging concerns raised through the grievance system.

BOM oversight of facilities and safety

Many of the underlying grievances that contribute to school tension — inadequate food, poor boarding facilities, overcrowding — are ultimately resource and infrastructure issues that require BOM-level decisions, not just principal-level management. A BOM that visits the school periodically, including unannounced visits to boarding facilities and dining halls, gains a far more accurate picture of conditions than one that only reviews written reports.

Practical BOM practice: Schools that have successfully maintained calm through periods of national tension often report that their BOM held more frequent meetings during those periods specifically, with student welfare as a standing agenda item — not just finance and infrastructure.

Parents as partners, not just recipients of information

Parents who are only contacted by the school when there is a problem — a fee arrears reminder, a disciplinary notice — develop a relationship with the school defined by negative interactions. Parents who receive regular, positive communication about their child's progress, and who are genuinely invited to raise concerns through structured channels like parent meetings, develop a more collaborative relationship that pays dividends when a difficult situation arises.

Listening to what parents hear at home

Parents often hear grievances from their children before the school administration becomes aware of them through internal channels. A school that actively invites parents to share what their children mention about school life — through parent meetings, a simple feedback channel, or direct outreach during high-tension periods — gains an additional, valuable source of early warning information.

Parent associations as a structured voice

A functioning Parents Association or Parent Teacher Association, meeting regularly and genuinely engaged by the principal, provides a structured channel for parent concerns to reach the administration collectively, rather than only individually. Principals who engage constructively with parent leadership — even when parent concerns are uncomfortable to hear — build a partnership that becomes valuable during difficult periods.

Communicating with parents during rising tension

When early warning signs of unrest are identified, proactive, honest communication with parents — not alarmist, but transparent — can enlist parents as allies in de-escalation. A message that says, in effect, "we are aware of some concerns among students, we are addressing them, and we would appreciate your support in reinforcing calm at home" treats parents as partners in the solution rather than only informing them after a crisis has occurred.

The BOM's role after an incident

Following any significant incident, the BOM should be involved in a genuine review — not to assign blame, but to understand what happened and what systemic changes are needed. A BOM that treats an incident purely as a communications problem to be managed, without genuine examination of underlying causes, misses the opportunity to prevent recurrence.
