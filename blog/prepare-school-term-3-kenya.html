How to prepare your school for Term 3 — the final push of the year

July 1, 2026 | 7 min read | By Edupath SMS Team

Term 3 is unlike any other term in the Kenyan school calendar. It is shorter, more intense, and carries the weight of the entire academic year. For Grade 12 students it is the final stretch before they leave the school for good. For Grade 10 and 11 students it determines whether they are ready for the year ahead. For the principal it is the term where every management decision made in Terms 1 and 2 either pays off or catches up.

Schools that enter Term 3 prepared consistently outperform those that treat it as a continuation of Term 2. Here is what that preparation looks like in practice.

Publish the timetable before the first day

The single most disruptive way to start Term 3 is with an incomplete or unpublished timetable. Students arrive not knowing where to go. Teachers arrive not knowing what they are teaching. The first week is wasted on logistics that should have been resolved in the holiday.

A Term 3 timetable should be finalised, updated in the digital system, and communicated to all teachers and students before the first day of term. In a school using Edupath SMS, publishing the timetable takes minutes — and every teacher and student sees their personalised schedule immediately from any device, without anyone having to photograph a notice board.

Review and close assessment gaps from Term 2

Before Term 3 begins, the deputy principal should run a report showing every student with incomplete or missing assessments from Term 2. These gaps need to be resolved in the first two weeks of Term 3 — after that, the assessment calendar for the current term takes over and there is no time to go back.

For Grade 12 students in particular, incomplete Term 2 assessment records are a serious risk. Identify them, contact the relevant subject teachers, and close the gaps before they compound.

Set clear Grade 12 priorities

Grade 12 teachers need to enter Term 3 with absolute clarity about what is left to cover, what assessments remain, and what the deadline is for each. A brief HOD-led meeting in the first week of term — reviewing scheme of work coverage and remaining assessment requirements — sets the right tone and prevents the scenario where a teacher discovers in October that they have not covered material that was needed in September.

Key date to anchor to: Work backwards from your school's end-of-term date for Grade 12 and confirm exactly how many teaching weeks remain per subject. Then verify that the remaining scheme of work can be covered in that time — and adjust if it cannot.

Send a strong opening communication to parents

A well-crafted opening WhatsApp message to all parents at the start of Term 3 sets expectations for the term and re-engages families who may have drifted during the holiday. Include: the term dates, the fee due date and M-Pesa payment link, any key events for the term, and a specific note for Grade 12 parents about what the term means for their child.

Schools that send this message at the start of Term 3 consistently report higher on-time fee payment rates and fewer attendance issues in the first two weeks — because parents have been reminded that school is back and fees are due.

Confirm staffing before the first day

Teacher absences at the start of Term 3 are more disruptive than at any other point in the year. Confirm with every teacher that they are returning, identify any expected absences, and have a substitution plan ready before the term begins. A school that discovers on the first Monday of Term 3 that two teachers have not returned is a school that has already lost the week.

Fee collection — start early

Term 3 fee collection windows are shorter because the term is shorter. A fee reminder sent to all parents in the last week of the holiday — with the exact balance outstanding and a direct M-Pesa payment link — consistently generates more on-time payments than any reminder sent after the term has started. Start the collection process before the term begins, not after.

Start Term 3 ready, not scrambling.
